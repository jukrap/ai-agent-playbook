# Product fit and direction

Record the user task and strongest product constraint before choosing decoration.

| Surface | Useful emphasis | Preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operations table | Comparison, filtering, fast scanning | Dense information and visible states |
| Editor | Content, tools and recovery | Working space and keyboard flow |
| Storefront | Inspectable products and decisions | Product identity, price and action clarity |
| Campaign or portfolio | Narrative and brand expression | Intentional color, geometry and motion |

For a reference, identify what is borrowed: hierarchy, spacing, density, type, or interaction. Separate that principle from its original brand assets. Define only the tokens, states, and responsive behavior needed for implementation; do not guess hidden data behavior from an image.

Example: keep a campaign's deliberate gradient while fixing low contrast. For an operations table, remove an oversized introductory hero if it hides the primary workflow. Neither choice is a universal ban on gradients or hero layouts.

Use existing preferences and accepted examples. Record a new durable preference only when the user actually selects it, not from one generated sample.
